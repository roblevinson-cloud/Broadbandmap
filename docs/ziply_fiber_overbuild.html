Method

Source. FCC Broadband Data Collection fixed broadband availability, as-of date 31 December 2025, state files for technology code 50 (fiber to the premises), all record types (residential, business and mixed-use).

Ziply Fiber. Ziply Fiber files as FCC provider ID 420173 (Northwest Fiber, LLC and affiliates; owned by BCE since August 2025); it is the only Ziply Fiber filer in the data. Its fiber footprint is 763,929 Broadband Serviceable Locations across 5 states.

Overlap. A Ziply Fiber passing counts as overbuilt when a rival reports technology 50 at the same location_id in the same filing. Cable, fixed wireless and DSL rivals are excluded — this is fiber-on-fiber only. Rival rows do not sum to the state total: one Ziply Fiber location can be overbuilt by several rivals, and the state total counts each location once.

Units and identity. Counts are BSLs, not housing units — one location can be a multi-unit building. Rivals are keyed by FCC provider ID rather than brand, so a filer using more than one brand name counts once — for example MontanaSky West LLC / WesternMontanaCommunityTel; inland / windwave; CenturyLink / Quantum Fiber; separate IDs stay separate even where they share a corporate parent.

Caveats. The ID carries three legal entities: Northwest Fiber, LLC (the ILEC, 693,300 locations), Ziply Fiber Pacific LLC (CLEC edge-outs, 70,824) and Northwest Fiber Management, LLC (the six Sheridan, Wyoming business locations bought from UPN). Every acquisition through 2025 (iFIBER, Ptera, EONI, LocalTel, the UPN Pacific Northwest assets) is filed under it. Since 1 August 2025 the company is owned by BCE (Bell Canada).

Washington is not what it looks like. The three largest “overbuilders” there — Chelan County PUD (23,369), Native Network (23,365) and Grant PUD (8,194) — are open-access public utility district networks on which Ziply is itself a retail tenant: the LocalTel footprint acquired in October 2024, first filed under Ziply's ID in this vintage. Those rows are the same physical fiber reported twice, not a rival building over Ziply. Of Washington's 43,946 overbuilt locations, 27,582 are overbuilt only by those three IDs; excluding them, Washington's fiber-overbuilt figure is 16,364 (3.9%) and the all-states total is roughly 97,400 (12.8%).
